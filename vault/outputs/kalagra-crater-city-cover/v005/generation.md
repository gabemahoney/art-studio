# Rogue Frontier — shared visual style

Create an original science-fiction adventure illustration in the vintage Star Frontiers cover-art style demonstrated by the supplied images in `gm/image_generation/ref_images/style_references/`. These actual images define the target more precisely than a decade label or the word “retro.” This is the campaign’s current art direction: the 70s illustration aesthetic represented by these Star Frontiers images.

## Drawing and paint

Use confident, visible drawing beneath opaque color: dark blue, brown, or near-black contours, sharp silhouette edges, angular internal marks, and selective fine hatching. Model forms with distinct painted light and shadow planes, small brushed highlights, and controlled soft blends. Combine illustrative precision on the subject with looser painted skies and distant terrain. Keep the handmade irregularities of painted commercial adventure art. Surface texture is subtle; do not cover everything in thick impasto, canvas noise, or an artificial distressed filter.

The result should read as a reproduced tabletop adventure cover painting. Avoid photographic rendering, 3D-rendered materials, modern cinematic concept art, lens blur, bloom, and uniformly hyper-detailed surfaces. Smooth painted gradients are appropriate in skies, glass and space; they must coexist with clear drawing and solid color shapes.

## Color and light

Use bold, distinct local colors: cobalt and ultramarine blue, cyan, scarlet, orange, yellow ochre, cream, and occasional green or violet as the scene requires. Organize them into a few dominant masses with purposeful contrasting accents. Preserve the subject’s established costume colors. Shadows can be deep navy, violet, green, or brown; highlights can be warm yellow or cool pale blue. Avoid a uniform brown wash or global teal-and-orange cinematic grade.

Light should clarify shape with deliberate painted highlights and strong value separations. Metal and glass may have bright, graphic reflective streaks. Dust and storms should retain distinct colored shapes and crisp focal silhouettes instead of submerging the entire image in desaturated haze.

## Composition and detail

Build around a strong, immediately readable focal silhouette, energetic diagonals, and clear overlapping foreground, middle distance, and background. For action, use expressive gestures and a concrete dramatic moment. For landscapes, let rock formations, architecture, storm bands, or lightning supply that energy without adding characters. Preserve breathing room and large quiet shapes around detail. Detail should describe selected forms rather than fill every surface with tiny mechanical marks.

## People and technology

Render people as individually recognizable, illustrated adventurers with convincing anatomy, expressive faces, clearly drawn hair, and simplified but dimensional skin. Keep approved age, build, facial structure, clothing and equipment. Dynamic cover-art staging does not authorize redesigning a known character or making everyone muscular or glamorous.

For new designs, use legible retro-futuristic forms: colored utility suits, boots, belts, goggles, compact equipment, boldly shaped hulls, panel lines, purposeful mechanical assemblies, and painted metal or plastic. Apply wear where the subject calls for it; universal grease, rust, military surplus, and dense kitbash detail are not style requirements. Architecture may use domes, towers, modular forms and practical industrial construction when appropriate to the scene. The references establish rendering, not campaign canon; do not import their characters, ships, symbols, or equipment into unrelated scenes.
