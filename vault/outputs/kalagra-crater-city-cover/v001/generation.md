- **Totalitarian Brutalism:** Vast, unadorned surfaces of poured reinforced concrete and blast-tempered steel alloys. Corridors are hyper-repetitive and axially rigid, punctuated by stark subterranean lighting slots and recessed control alcoves. The scale intentionally dwarfs the human occupant, turning institutional power into an imposing physical boundary. Star Wars Imperial architecture is a reference for scale and institutional severity only; invent Rogue Frontier’s own layouts and symbols.
    
- **Vernacular Adaptation (Frontier Settlements):** Subterranean dwellings dug out of compressed sandstone (with Matmata, Tunisia, as an architectural reference), sunken courtyards with atmospheric water collectors, and sun-bleached adobe vaults. Openings are minimal to limit heat gain, and high-tech conduits are threaded crudely into porous stone walls. Star Wars Outer Rim settlements are mood references only; create original settlement plans and equipment.
    
- **Industrial Modularism (Outposts and Shipyards):** Prefabricated bulkhead modules, corrugated metal shelters, and shipping containers converted into permanent housing. **Material Culture & Everyday Objects**

- **Hardware Assembly:** Everyday gear is heavy, cast-metal, and modular. Blasters feature knurled grips, optical sight brackets adapted from camera lenses and scopes, and exposed wiring bundles secured with duct clamps. There are almost no molded, seamless plastic unibodies; everything appears bolted, welded, or clamped together from pre-existing stock parts.

# ART STYLE

Use a **retro 1970s, painterly, semi-realistic science fiction illustration style suitable for premium tabletop roleplaying game art**.

Characters should have:

* believable human anatomy
* individual facial structures
* expressive faces
* naturally imperfect features
* realistic fabric behavior
* detailed handmade clothing and equipment

The image should remain clearly **illustrated rather than photographic**.

The art style combines mid-century industrial technical illustration with classic mythic romanticism. **Ralph McQuarrie**, **Joe Johnston**, and **Colin Cantwell** are visual reference points for functional design, painted atmosphere, and memorable silhouettes; create original Rogue Frontier designs from those broad principles.

**Core Visual Pillars**

- **Aerospace Technical Draftsmanship:** Treat vehicles and installations as functional engineering assemblies: coherent perspective, draughtsman-line clarity, logical weight-bearing structures, and minimal decorative filigree. Use technical views when appropriate for reference sheets and natural scene perspective for illustrations.
    
- **Gouache, Matte, and Vignetted Light:** The foundational concept paintings and original matte backdrops relied heavily on gouache and acrylic washes. Compositions favor broad horizons, heavy atmospheric dust, bleached atmospheric perspective (dust haze, particulate occlusion), and theatrical rim-lighting that emphasizes silhouettes over surface sheen.
    
- **Read-at-a-Distance Silhouettes:** Every starship, robot, and costume is designed around a strong, distinct geometric profile. Combine proportions, masses, and negative space into original silhouettes rather than recreating recognizable Star Wars designs. If blacked out into a silhouette, a recurring subject should remain identifiable against starfields or open desert skies.
    
- **Kitbash Texture (Greebling):** Surfaces reject smooth digital finishes in favor of dense, tactile visual noise borrowed from model kits: tank tracks, carburetor manifolds, and exposed cooling fins layered onto simple base geometries to suggest immense scale and functional complexity.

Avoid plastic-smooth digital faces.

Avoid fashion photography.

Avoid glossy cinematic superhero aesthetics.

The characters should look like **particular people**, not idealized science fiction casting models.
